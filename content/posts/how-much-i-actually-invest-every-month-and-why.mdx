I asked a dozen people how much to invest per month before I ever put money in myself, and every answer was either a vague percentage or an amount clearly meant for someone earning far more than I was. So I picked a number that felt uncomfortable but not impossible, and adjusted from there.

Right now I invest $420 a month, which is roughly 12 percent of my take home pay. That number didn't start there, and it isn't meant to be a target for you to copy exactly. What follows is how I got to it and why it keeps changing.

## Why a Universal Percentage Never Quite Fit

Most advice lands on 15 percent as a general target, and it's a reasonable starting benchmark if you have nothing else to go on. But 15 percent of my income in my first working year would have meant skipping rent some months, which makes the advice technically correct and practically useless at the same time.

The number that actually works is the one that survives contact with your real bills, not the one that sounds responsible on a finance blog.

## How My Monthly Investment Amount Actually Grew

### Year One: $50 a Month, and That Was a Genuine Stretch

I started at $50 a month while carrying student loan debt and an entry level salary. It felt small compared to advice telling me to invest 15 percent, but $50 automated and consistent beat a 15 percent target I would have abandoned within two months.

### Year Two: $150 a Month, After a Raise and Paid Off Credit Card

Once a raise landed and a small credit card balance cleared, I increased my contribution instead of letting my spending quietly absorb the extra income. This is the habit that actually mattered more than the specific dollar figure.

### Year Three: $420 a Month, My Current Number

This reflects a further raise, a fully funded emergency fund that freed up money previously going toward that cushion, and a conscious decision to keep my lifestyle roughly flat while my income kept climbing. It's about 12 percent of my current take home pay, below the often cited 15 percent benchmark, and I'm fine with that for reasons I'll get into.

## Why I'm Not at 15 Percent Yet, on Purpose

I'm still paying down a portion of a car loan, and I'd rather hit that number honestly at 12 percent while making real progress on debt than force 15 percent and neglect a balance carrying real interest. Once that loan clears, the plan is to redirect that payment straight into my investment contribution, which should push me past 15 percent without feeling like a fresh sacrifice.

This is my honest opinion on the matter: chasing a benchmark percentage while ignoring higher interest debt is often the wrong tradeoff, not the disciplined one it's sometimes framed as.

## What I'd Actually Tell You to Do

Don't start with a percentage. Start with an amount that's genuinely automatic and genuinely survivable on your current income, even if that number embarrasses you compared to advice aimed at someone further along than you are. Increase it every time your income increases, before your spending has a chance to absorb the difference first.

## The Honest Risk Worth Naming

However much you decide to invest, remember that markets fluctuate and none of this guarantees a specific outcome by a specific date. Only invest what you can genuinely leave alone for several years, and keep your emergency fund separate from whatever number you land on here, since investments aren't a substitute for accessible cash in a real emergency.

How much to invest per month was never a fixed answer for me. It was a number that grew honestly alongside my income, my debt, and what I could actually sustain without quietly resenting the plan by month three.
